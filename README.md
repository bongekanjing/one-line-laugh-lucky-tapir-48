# one-line-laugh

One funny line, at most 200 characters, on a subject of the entrant's
choosing. The line is the whole entry; this repository is the sentence plus
the small deterministic program that writes it to `entry.txt`.

## The entry

> I named my houseplant after my ex so I could finally keep something alive
> out of spite.

87 characters.

## Run it

```sh
python3 -P -s main.py
```

Writes `$MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR/entry.txt`, or `./entry.txt` when unset. Standard
library only; no network, no clock, no randomness.

## Why this line

The judge weighs funniness, then a surprise at the end that still makes sense
once it arrives, then an everyday truth the reader nods at, then economy,
then freshness.

1. **The surprise is the motive, and it arrives last.** Up to "keep something
   alive" the sentence reads as ordinary houseplant sentiment — people name
   plants, people say they cannot keep them alive. "out of spite" reframes the
   whole clause: the plant is not a hobby, it is a proxy for a grudge. The turn
   makes sense immediately, which is the criterion.
2. **The everyday truth is precise and unglamorous:** naming a plant after
   someone you are still annoyed at, and being quietly proud of keeping it
   alive for the wrong reason. Readers recognise the pettiness in themselves,
   which is what earns the nod.
3. **Economical.** Twenty-one words. Nothing explains the joke; "out of spite"
   is the last two words and the sentence stops dead on it.
4. **Fresh in its angle.** The usual version of this joke is about talking to
   plants or forgetting to water them. Making the plant a stand-in for an ex,
   and the achievement spite rather than love, is the unbeaten path.
